## What this is

When a provider handles personal information on my behalf, there should be a contract that says what they may do with it, how they protect it and what happens if something goes wrong. That contract is a data processing agreement (DPA).

## Providers that handle personal information for this site

| Provider | What it handles | Agreement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cloudflare | Visitor request data, to host and protect the site | Cloudflare's standard DPA, which is part of its self-serve terms |
| Proton Mail | Emails you send me | Proton's terms and privacy policy. A separate DPA is **not yet confirmed**. |
| Gumroad | Buyer details and payments | Gumroad's terms. Gumroad is the seller of record for purchases, so it's responsible for payment data in its own right. |

## Providers that don't receive visitor data

GitHub (code, builds and the status page) and Substack (where posts are written) don't receive anything about visitors to this site. If you visit them directly, you're dealing with them under their own terms.

## For clients

If a service means I handle personal information that belongs to **your** business (for example, seeing user lists during a Microsoft 365 review), I'm the processor and you're the controller. We put that in writing before work starts: what I'll access, for how long, how it's protected, and that it's deleted afterwards.

## If this changes

Before any new provider touches personal information:

1. Check they offer a DPA and accept it. If they don't offer one, don't use them for personal data.
2. Check where they store data and what covers transfers out of Canada or the EU.
3. Check their own sub-processors.
4. Add them to the table above and to [Third-party disclosure](/legal/third-party-disclosure/).
